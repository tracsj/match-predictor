# Pre-registration: re-analysing Phase 6's closing-line value against a measured null

**Committed 2026-08-17, before Phase 6's bet population had been re-derived and before any matched null was computed for it.**

## Why this needs a pre-registration at all

It is a re-analysis of a settled study, not a search for edge. There is one bet population, one metric, and no threshold to tune, so the usual way a backtest manufactures a result is not available here.

**It gets one anyway, for a different reason: the answer is flattering.** `docs/PHASE6_RESULT.md` concluded that the model's selections "sat on the wrong side of the market's own movement." The preliminary measurement in `scripts/clv_null_calibration.py` suggests the opposite, and overturning a published negative in the direction of *the model was better than we said* is exactly the circumstance in which a null gets chosen to suit. Fixing the null's definition before computing it is the whole protection.

## The claim under test

`docs/PHASE6_RESULT.md` reports CLV of **0.9952 mean ratio at 42.4% shortened** on 1,337 Pinnacle pre-close bets, and reads it as evidence the selections were on the wrong side of the market's movement.

That reading is valid only if the null is a mean ratio of 1.0 and a shortening rate of 50%. Measured, it is not: Pinnacle's overround tightens toward kickoff, so prices lengthen by default.

## What is fixed here, before anything runs

### 1. The bet population — re-derived, not approximated

Phase 6's own bets, regenerated by running its own code: `build_holdout()` then `fit_and_predict()` from `src/phase6.py`, the settled `NetConfig`, `ALL_FEATURES`, three seeds (0, 1, 2), temperature-scaled on the training tail. Then `simulate()` with the pre-registered `BetRule(min_ev=0.05, min_odds=1.5, max_odds=5.0, stake=1.0)` against each pre-close price set.

**The reproduction gate, and it is a gate rather than a report.** The regenerated Pinnacle pre-close population must reproduce `docs/PHASE6_RESULT.md`'s published row — **1,337 bets, mean ratio 0.9952, 42.4% shortened** — to within tolerance. Fixed in advance: **bet count within ±2%, mean ratio within ±0.002, shortening rate within ±0.01.**

**If it does not reproduce, the re-analysis stops and reports the discrepancy.** A null computed against a population that is not Phase 6's would answer a question nobody asked, and "close enough" decided after seeing the numbers is how that failure gets waved through.

### 2. The null — one definition, fixed now

For each price set, the null is the shortening rate of selections drawn from the **same season, same divisions, same rule-eligible cells**: every (match, outcome) whose pre-close price for that price set falls in [1.5, 5.0] and which carries both a finite pre-close and a finite Pinnacle close for that same outcome.

**Odds-matched**, by ten deciles taken from the model's own bets in that price set, 200 sims, using `matched_null` from `scripts/h1_odds_matched_null.py` unchanged. A decile with no eligible cells is skipped rather than filled from a neighbour.

Reported for each price set: the model's observed rate, the matched null with a 95% interval, the margin, and a one-proportion z.

### 3. What counts as overturning the published reading

**Primary, Pinnacle pre-close only** — the column Phase 6 itself led with:

- **Overturned** if the observed shortening rate lies **above** the matched null's 95% interval. The published "wrong side of the market's movement" reading is then wrong, and `docs/PHASE6_RESULT.md` gets a correction rather than a flag.
- **Upheld** if the observed rate lies **below** the interval.
- **Inconclusive** if it lies inside it — in which case the honest statement is that Phase 6's CLV distinguishes nothing about direction, which is itself a correction to a page that read it as a clear negative.

The other three pre-close columns (Bet365, market average, market maximum) are reported in the same table and are **descriptive**. They cannot overturn or rescue the primary. Market maximum in particular already exceeded 1.0 in the published table and was correctly dismissed there as price shopping; a matched null does not change what that column is.

### 4. What this cannot do, stated in advance

**It cannot make Phase 6 profitable, and no result here should be presented as softening its conclusion.** The ROI tables stand untouched: the rule lost money in every price column, and the losses exceeded the random-bet null. A CLV direction is not a yield. The most this re-analysis can establish is that the model's *price selection* was better than recorded while its *outcome selection* remained worse than the market's — which is precisely the pattern H1's diagnostics already found, at −4.69% ROI.

**It does not revisit `docs/PREREGISTRATION.md`.** That file is a frozen record and is not edited retroactively, exactly as the registry's own note about the 38-vs-47 count discrepancy says.

### 5. Registry treatment

**This does not increment the configuration count.** No configuration is chosen, scored or compared: it recomputes a statistic on an existing, fixed bet population from an already-counted run. It is the same category as `h1_holdout_tiers.py` re-slicing Phase 6 by tier — a new cut of a counted result.

The count stays at **48**, and this file is the record of why.

## The expected outcome, written down in advance

**The published reading is overturned.** The preliminary unconditional measurement put the null at 38.89% against Phase 6's observed 42.40%, which is roughly +3.5pp and about z = 2.6. The odds-matched null is expected to land close to the unconditional one — in the H1 population the two agreed to within 0.002 — so the margin is expected to survive matching.

**The confidence is deliberately moderate.** That agreement was measured on different seasons and different divisions, and 2025-26 has the most extreme drift in the corpus, so a matched null could plausibly move further than it did in the H1 window. If the margin does not survive matching, that is the result and it gets written exactly as it comes out.
